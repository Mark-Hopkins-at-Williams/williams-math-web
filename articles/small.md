The SMALL Undergraduate Research Program is a nine-week residential summer program in which undergraduates investigate open research problems in mathematics. One of the largest programs of its kind in the country, SMALL is supported by an [NSF grant for REUs](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/initiatives/reu) and by the Science Center of Williams College. Over 800 students have participated in the program since its inception in 1988. (SMALL is an acronym from the names of the founding faculty: Silva, Morgan, Adams, Lenhart, Levine.)

During SMALL, students work in groups directed by individual faculty members. Many participants have published papers based on their SMALL projects (see the publications list below) and have subsequently gone on to complete PhDs in Mathematics. Students also enjoy the attractions of summer in the Berkshires: hiking, biking, plays, concerts, etc. Weekly lunches, teas, and casual sporting events bring SMALL students together with faculty and others doing research at Williams College.

The SMALL program has a storied history. Some notable achievements include progress on the [double bubble conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bubble_theorem#History) which led to its ultimate resolution, as well as the classification of all the (non-edge-to-edge) regular polygonal [tilings of the sphere](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1007/s00454-024-00689-z).
